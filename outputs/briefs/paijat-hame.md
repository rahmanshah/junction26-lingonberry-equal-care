# Päijät-Häme: lung cancer pathway brief

Status: **validated** · gemini-4-argon (thinking HIGH) · 2026-11-14T12:56:00+00:00 · attempts: 1

## Where do we lag?

**Päijät-Häme lags in crude MDT recording at 0.3% (95% CI 0.1–1.1) versus 6.0% nationally, which measures recording practice rather than care.** `cty:mdt_recorded:Päijät_Hämeen_hyvinvointialue, nat:mdt_recorded, bs:coverage:MDT`

- For curative treatment, the county's crude share is 29.6% (95% CI 26.2–33.2; n=195/659), which is not distinguishable from the national 27.0% (4009/14873) because the 95% confidence interval includes the national value. `cty:curative:Päijät_Hämeen_hyvinvointialue, nat:curative`
- Because treatment categories overlap (a patient can have several), Päijät-Häme's crude shares are above national levels for life-prolonging treatment at 44.5% (95% CI 40.7–48.3; n=293/659) versus 30.4% (4522/14873) and symptomatic treatment at 50.8% (95% CI 47.0–54.6; n=335/659) versus 39.5% (5871/14873), as the 95% confidence intervals exclude the national values. `cty:life_prolonging:Päijät_Hämeen_hyvinvointialue, nat:life_prolonging, cty:symptomatic:Päijät_Hämeen_hyvinvointialue, nat:symptomatic, bs:coverage:Treatment_start`
- In multidisciplinary team (MDT) coding, the county's crude recorded share is 0.3% (95% CI 0.1–1.1; n=2/659), where the 95% confidence interval excludes and falls below the national 6.0% (893/14873), though HILMO entries come only from direct patient contacts so MDT records measure recording practice, not care. `cty:mdt_recorded:Päijät_Hämeen_hyvinvointialue, nat:mdt_recorded, bs:coverage:MDT, q:39e6ba43a9`
- Public registry data do not measure county waiting times for referrals or treatment start, while preliminary, unpublished figures show lung cancer care in Finland takes 155 days on average from initial symptoms to treatment (56 days patient delay, 41 days primary care, and 58 days specialist care) compared with 107 in Sweden and 67 in Denmark. `bs:coverage:Referral, bs:coverage:Treatment_start, mot:total_fi, mot:patient, mot:primary, mot:specialist, mot:total_se, mot:total_dk`

## How sure are we?

**Päijät-Häme's crude no-treatment-record share is 17.8% (95% CI 15.0–20.9) versus 27.5% nationally, suggesting relatively complete treatment recording, though county shares are crude and pathway timestamps are absent.** `cty:no_treatment_record:Päijät_Hämeen_hyvinvointialue, nat:no_treatment_record, bs:coverage:Treatment_start`

- The crude share with no treatment record within 12 months (mixing untreated patients and unrecorded treatment) is 17.8% (95% CI 15.0–20.9; n=117/659), whose 95% confidence interval is entirely below the national 27.5% (4089/14873); as an inference, not a finding, this signal is consistent with relatively complete treatment recording, making a low curative share less likely to be only a recording artefact. `cty:no_treatment_record:Päijät_Hämeen_hyvinvointialue, nat:no_treatment_record, bs:coverage:Treatment_start`
- County shares are crude with separate limitations: they are not age-adjusted (age-adjusted values exist only in Kuva 16 for curative treatment by sex, digitised from a figure at about ±0.5 percentage points, with national reference lines of 26.7% for men and 26.9% for women), and separately cannot be stage-adjusted because stage at diagnosis is missing for about 48% of patients. `k16:curative:men:national, k16:curative:women:national, bs:coverage:Diagnosis, q:7885b0974f`
- A journal article found hospital discharge register (HILMO) diagnoses match the Cancer Registry for about 90% of most cancer types, which concerns diagnosis concordance only and says nothing about treatment, referral or MDT recording, while Finland has no cancer quality registry and lung cancer data reporting, collection and storage remain incomplete. `lit:hilmo_90, filha:registry, q:47c7e11303, q:950a2f797b`
- Public data omit symptom onset, referral dates, treatment start dates, and imaging waits (reporting only that about 20% are diagnosed by clinical examination alone, such as imaging); outpatient primary care visits are not included in the report's data, occupational health is not mentioned, and HILMO shows 93.6% had a specialist visit within 12 months without visit dates. `bs:coverage:Symptoms, bs:coverage:Referral, bs:coverage:Treatment_start, bs:coverage:Imaging, q:5eb155decc, bs:coverage:Primary_care_occupational_health, q:323de0d90d, bs:coverage:First_specialist_visit`

## What should we do first?

**The team recommends auditing MDT, stage and drug-treatment recording completeness, tracking access times using THL 21/2016 definitions with handover alerts, and standardising referrals with a named diagnostic contact.** `rec:3, rec:1, rec:2, bs:coverage:MDT, bs:coverage:Diagnosis, thl:21_2016`

- Because Päijät-Häme's crude recorded MDT share is 0.3% (95% CI 0.1–1.1; n=2/659) versus 6.0% (893/14873) nationally—measuring recording practice, not care—and stage is missing for about 48% of patients, the team recommends publishing county recording completeness for stage, drug treatment and MDT coding while the STM roadmap (measure 3.2.1) plans a national quality registry (2026–2028 decree; 2029–2031 reports) at 90% coverage. `rec:3, cty:mdt_recorded:Päijät_Hämeen_hyvinvointialue, nat:mdt_recorded, bs:coverage:MDT, q:39e6ba43a9, bs:coverage:Diagnosis, q:7885b0974f, q:f07a34a300, q:4a856bd695, q:f540c706f8`
- Since Health Care Act §52 sets no maximum time for a cancer suspicion and preliminary national figures show 58 days in specialist care, the team recommends reporting access times using THL guidance Ohjaus 21/2016 definitions (for 16 cancers) with handover alerts while the STM roadmap (measure 3.1.2) plans national pathway descriptions and access times by 2026–2028. `rec:1, law:no_max, mot:specialist, thl:21_2016, q:24169121a4, q:def306014c, q:b326e35410`
- Because referrals follow local instructions and mentor insight notes a weak referral gets a lower urgency rating, the team recommends a standard referral template, pulmonologist referral for persistent symptoms after a normal X-ray, and a named diagnostic-phase contact while the STM roadmap plans standardised referral criteria (measure 2.5.2) and named care-coordinator guidance (measure 1.2.3) by 2026–2028. `rec:2, q:556431a0c2, mentor:kaisa_referral, q:9a1cd194f5, q:c61439b30f, q:fc81032a80, q:abcbb06ae3, q:050ee09ebe`
- Separately, the Filha National Lung Cancer Programme's national lung cancer diagnostics recommendation—which includes referral criteria for primary care and recommended time limits for specialist care—is to be carried out before the 2030 mid-term evaluation (with final evaluation in 2036), supporting the team's recommendation to establish local referral rules and access-time monitoring now. `rec:1, rec:2, q:8f358a9de6, q:29b792498e, q:432c0e51d6, q:614febebf2`
