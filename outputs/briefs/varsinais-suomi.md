# Varsinais-Suomi: lung cancer pathway brief

Status: **validated** · gemini-4-argon (thinking HIGH) · 2026-11-14T11:08:09+00:00 · attempts: 1

## Where we lag

- In Varsinais-Suomen hyvinvointialue, the crude share of new lung cancer patients receiving curative treatment within 12 months is 24.3% (95% CI 22.1–26.6; n=349/1437) compared with 27.0% (4009/14873) nationally, and the 95% confidence interval excludes the national value. `cty:curative:Varsinais_Suomen_hyvinvointialue, nat:curative`
- Across overlapping treatment categories where a patient can receive several treatments, the county's crude shares are also below national values at 9.7% (95% CI 8.3–11.4; n=140/1437) versus 30.4% (4522/14873) nationally for life-prolonging treatment and 30.8% (95% CI 28.5–33.3; n=443/1437) versus 39.5% (5871/14873) nationally for symptomatic treatment, with 95% confidence intervals excluding the national value for each. `cty:life_prolonging:Varsinais_Suomen_hyvinvointialue, nat:life_prolonging, cty:symptomatic:Varsinais_Suomen_hyvinvointialue, nat:symptomatic`
- The crude share of patients with no treatment record within 12 months—a category that mixes untreated patients and treatment that was not recorded—is 43.1% (95% CI 40.5–45.6; n=619/1437) in the county compared with 27.5% (4089/14873) nationally, and the 95% confidence interval excludes the national value. `cty:no_treatment_record:Varsinais_Suomen_hyvinvointialue, nat:no_treatment_record, bs:coverage:Treatment_start`
- A multidisciplinary team (MDT) meeting is recorded for 1.6% (95% CI 1.1–2.4; n=23/1437) of patients in the county versus 6.0% (893/14873) nationally, with the 95% confidence interval excluding the national value, although this indicator reflects HILMO recording practice from direct patient contacts rather than actual multidisciplinary review. `cty:mdt_recorded:Varsinais_Suomen_hyvinvointialue, nat:mdt_recorded, bs:coverage:MDT, q:39e6ba43a9`

## How sure are we

- The county treatment shares are crude—not adjusted for age or stage, as stage at diagnosis is missing for ~48% of new patients—and in figures digitised from Kuva 16 (precision ±0.5 percentage points), the lower curative share holds after age adjustment for men at 22.7% in the county versus 26.7% nationally, though the 95% confidence interval bar could not be read because it is hidden inside the dot (half-width < ~3.3 percentage points). `bs:coverage:Diagnosis, q:7885b0974f, k16:curative:men:Varsinais_Suomen_hyvinvointialue, k16:curative:men:national`
- For women, the curative gap does not hold after age adjustment in the digitised Kuva 16 figures (precision ±0.5 percentage points), where the county's age-adjusted curative share is 27.4% (95% CI 23.1–31.8) compared with the national reference line of 26.9%, and the 95% confidence interval includes the national value. `k16:curative:women:Varsinais_Suomen_hyvinvointialue, k16:curative:women:national`
- Because the county's 43.1% (95% CI 40.5–45.6; n=619/1437) share with no treatment record within 12 months exceeds the 27.5% (4089/14873) national share, it serves as a data-quality signal that the low recorded shares of curative, life-prolonging and symptomatic treatment may partly reflect unrecorded care, even though a journal article found that hospital discharge register (Hilmo) diagnoses matched the Cancer Registry for about 90% of most cancer types while referral and first-procedure data reliability remains unconfirmed. `cty:no_treatment_record:Varsinais_Suomen_hyvinvointialue, nat:no_treatment_record, bs:coverage:Treatment_start, lit:hilmo_90, q:950a2f797b`
- Public registry data cannot show where patients wait along the pathway because they omit symptom onset, primary care and occupational health visits, imaging dates and waits (with clinical examination such as imaging serving as the sole basis of diagnosis in approximately 20% of cases), referral dates, county-level timing of the first specialist visit (recorded within 12 months for 93.6% of patients nationally) and exact treatment start dates. `bs:coverage:Symptoms, bs:coverage:Primary_care_occupational_health, q:323de0d90d, bs:coverage:Imaging, q:5eb155decc, bs:coverage:Referral, bs:coverage:First_specialist_visit, bs:coverage:Treatment_start`
- Preliminary, unpublished figures indicate that lung cancer care in Finland takes 155 days on average from first symptoms to first treatment—compared with 107 days in Sweden and 67 days in Denmark—broken down in preliminary estimates into 56 days of patient delay, 41 days in primary care and 58 days in specialist care, which is the longest stage. `mot:total_fi, mot:total_se, mot:total_dk, mot:patient, mot:primary, mot:specialist`

## What to do first

- Given that 43.1% (95% CI 40.5–45.6; n=619/1437) of patients in Varsinais-Suomen hyvinvointialue have no treatment record within 12 months, 1.6% (95% CI 1.1–2.4; n=23/1437) have an MDT meeting recorded (codes WZC15/WZC20) and stage at diagnosis is missing for ~48% of patients nationally, the county should first audit and publish the recording completeness of stage, cancer treatments and MDT meetings under uniform recording instructions, in preparation for the planned national cancer quality registry targeting 90% data coverage. `cty:no_treatment_record:Varsinais_Suomen_hyvinvointialue, cty:mdt_recorded:Varsinais_Suomen_hyvinvointialue, bs:coverage:MDT, bs:coverage:Diagnosis, q:7885b0974f, q:950a2f797b, q:4c7a01d84c, q:990a0d39a0, filha:registry, q:f07a34a300, q:4a856bd695`
- Because the Health Care Act (§52) sets no legal maximum time for a cancer suspicion, national diagnostic schedules are not yet uniform and stated times in regional pathway documents go unmonitored while preliminary data show 58 days in specialist care, the county should report access times across handovers (referral, first specialist visit, diagnosis and treatment start) using THL guidance Ohjaus 21/2016 definitions for 16 cancers and flag stalled handovers until national target times are defined in 2026–2028 and implemented before the 2030 interim evaluation. `law:no_max, q:971b2f468f, reg:not_monitored, mot:specialist, thl:21_2016, q:24169121a4, q:cd51b9d5b6, q:def306014c, q:432c0e51d6, q:614febebf2`
- To speed up entry into and progression through specialist care—where mentor insight indicates that a weak referral receives a lower urgency rating in specialist care while occupational health gets patients to specialist care faster—the county should adopt standardised primary care referral criteria, a referral template (covering history, medications, pack-years, asbestos exposure, functional capacity and what the patient has been told) and a rule to refer persistent unexplained symptoms to a pulmonologist even when an X-ray is clear, as set out in a regional pathway document, alongside a named contact person for the diagnostic phase (which regional pathway documents currently lack) before national guidelines arrive in 2026–2028. `mentor:kaisa_referral, mentor:hanna_occupational, q:37bb4c3b38, q:29b792498e, q:9a1cd194f5, reg:kys_template, reg:kys_persistent, reg:only_kys_xray, reg:named_contact, q:fc81032a80, q:74a8cee731, q:abcbb06ae3`
