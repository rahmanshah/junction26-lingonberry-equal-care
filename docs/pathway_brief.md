# Lung cancer care pathway in Finland, with diabetes for comparison

*Lingonberry / Junction 2026, background brief for Shah. Prepared by Hellu, 14 Nov 2026.*

---

## 1. Key points

- Finland has a **National Lung Cancer Programme 2026–2035** (Filha, May 2026), but it has **no national care pathway and no time targets in days**. Those will come in a separate diagnostic recommendation, due before the 2030 mid-term evaluation.
- With no national pathway, pathways are defined **regionally**, and they differ. Of the five regions we reviewed, two state times for the diagnostic phase (Keski-Suomi and Pirha), one only addresses a normal X-ray with continuing symptoms (KYS), and none says whether its times are monitored.
- THL published **national rules for recording and monitoring cancer access times** in 2016 (in force since 1 Jan 2017, covering 16 cancers). We found no published cancer-specific report based on them.
- The law sets **no maximum time for a cancer suspicion**. Only the general non-urgent limits apply.
- On average, lung cancer takes **155 days from first symptoms to treatment** in Finland, compared with 107 in Sweden and 67 in Denmark (preliminary data). Specialist care is the longest stage at 58 days.
- Finland has **no cancer quality registry**. Referral, first-visit and diagnosis timestamps exist in hospital systems, but nobody monitors them.

---

## 2. What a care pathway is

A care pathway (*hoitopolku*, or *hoitoketju* at regional level) sets out who does what at each step: primary care, specialist care, and how a patient moves between them. In Finland, the wellbeing services counties write these pathways.

---

## 3. National level: a programme, but no pathway or time targets

| Document | What it says |
|---|---|
| National Cancer Strategy 2026–2035, p.33 | Finland has no national system for cancer-specific care guidelines or pathways |
| Roadmap 2026–2031, measure 3.1.2, p.56 | National pathways and access target times for five cancers to be defined in 2026–2028, modelled on Denmark, Sweden and Norway |
| National Lung Cancer Programme 2026–2035 (Filha) | An action plan, not a treatment recommendation. Covers the whole pathway from symptoms to palliative care, but gives no day-based targets for referral, diagnosis or treatment. Notes that Finland has had no precise national guidance on the timing of diagnostic steps |

Further points from the National Lung Cancer Programme:
- It reports large regional variation in diagnostic speed, referral organisation and surgery volumes.
- Referral criteria for primary care will be set out in the upcoming diagnostic recommendation.
- Finland has no lung cancer or other cancer quality registry. The Cancer Registry, THL and FICAN are assessing what registry is needed, starting with lung cancer.
- Monitoring will rely on annual indicators, hospital surveys at the start and end of the programme, and registry survival data. It does **not** monitor pathway timestamps.

## 4. Diabetes vs lung cancer suspicion

| | Type 2 diabetes | Lung cancer suspicion |
|---|---|---|
| National guideline | Käypä hoito since 2007 | National programme since 2026, but no pathway or time targets yet |
| Follow-up | Scheduled (HbA1c every 6–12 months, yearly kidney and lipid tests, eye screening) | None before diagnosis |
| Named contact | Diabetes nurse | Usually nobody until specialist care accepts the patient |
| Who starts the next step | The system books follow-ups | Often the patient (returning if symptoms continue) |
| National quality register | No | No |

Diabetes is a chronic disease with a routine, so the system keeps track of the patient. Lung cancer suspicion is a one-off chain of handovers. Each handover can drop the patient, and nobody owns the whole chain.

---

## 5. What the law guarantees (Health Care Act, §52)

| Clock | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 3 weeks | Specialist care must **start** assessing the need for care after the referral arrives |
| 3 months | If a specialist assessment or special imaging or lab tests are needed, the assessment and tests must be done |
| 6 months | Treatment must start, "within a reasonable time considering urgency" |

- Valvira notes that the 3-week rule leaves the **maximum** time for the assessment open. The assessment can start just by a specialist reading the referral, without seeing the patient.
- **Urgent care** (care that cannot be postponed without harm) must be given immediately. A cancer suspicion is triaged as "urgent" inside the hospital, but legally it is not emergency care.
- **Result:** there is no legal maximum time for a cancer suspicion. All measured delays fall within the legal limits, even when they are clinically long.

## 6. Regional pathways compared

All documents below were read in full on 14 Nov 2026.

| Region | Document type | Diagnostic-phase times stated | Monitoring of times | Normal X-ray, symptoms continue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Keski-Suomi** (HyvaKS) | Patient pathway web page (no date) | Referral handled "as quickly as possible"; **target: lung clinic appointment within 2–3 weeks, at the latest about 1 month after the referral arrives**; tissue sample results in about 1–2 weeks | Not mentioned | Not addressed |
| **Pirha / Tays** (Tampere) | Patient pathway web page (no date) | Referral handled "usually within 1–2 working days"; invitation "usually within 3–4 working days"; CT "most often within a week"; bronchoscopy results "usually within 3–5 days" | Not mentioned | Not addressed |
| **KYS** (Pohjois-Savo) | **Professional care chain** for staff (v1.1, 08/2021) | None. Referral urgency class R1, with no time attached | Not mentioned | **Addressed:** persistent unexplained symptoms → refer for pulmonologist assessment even if the X-ray is clear |
| **HUS** (Uusimaa) | Patient pathway web page (Finnish updated 15 Jun 2026) | None | Not mentioned | Not addressed |
| **OYS / Pohde** (Pohjois-Pohjanmaa) | Patient pathway web page (last modified 17 Sep 2025) | None. Investigations are "urgent but not emergency", and access "may vary depending on the referral situation" | Not mentioned | Not addressed |
| **Satakunta** | Pulmonary clinic web page only; no lung cancer pathway published | None | Not mentioned | Not addressed |
| *Satakunta (research, University of Turku)* | *Regional study* | *Measured: referral made in about two weeks; referral → diagnosis just under two months; biggest delay in specialist care* | n/a | n/a |

**Other differences**
- **MDT:** KYS reviews every patient in a weekly MDT. Keski-Suomi reviews every new case. HUS refers to the MDT only "when needed".
- **Referral content:** KYS is the only region that lists what a referral must contain (history, medications, pack-years, asbestos exposure, functional capacity, what the patient has been told) and provides a referral template. This responds directly to Kaisa's point about referral quality.
- **Named contact:** no region names a contact person for the diagnostic phase. HUS assigns a nurse at the first visit; Keski-Suomi mentions a treatment coordinator at the surgery clinic.
- **Primary care role:** OYS and Keski-Suomi explicitly include occupational health as a referral source.

**What this shows**
- Every region describes the same basic steps: GP → X-ray → urgent referral → pulmonary clinic → MDT.
- The timing differs: Keski-Suomi's target allows up to about a month to a first appointment, while Pirha describes CT within roughly a week. Three regions give no times at all.
- All stated times are targets or typical times, not commitments, and no region says whether it meets them.
- Only KYS tells primary care what to do when the X-ray is normal but symptoms continue. Elsewhere, this is the point where the patient is left alone.
- Only KYS standardises referral content.
- From the patient's side, the waiting time is mostly invisible.

*Limitation: five regions plus one regional study, not all 22 areas. KYS's document is aimed at professionals; the others are aimed at patients, so they are not fully comparable.*

**National monitoring exists on paper.** THL's guidance *Hoitoonpääsyn seuranta syövän hoidossa* (Ohjaus 21/2016, in force since 1 Jan 2017) defined how cancer access times are recorded for 16 cancers, so that they could be reported nationally. A 2019 study in Lääkärilehti found that hospital discharge register (Hilmo) diagnoses matched the Cancer Registry for about 90% of most cancer types. It also noted that the reliability of referral and first-procedure data still needed to be confirmed. We found no published cancer-specific access-time report → **open question for mentors**.

## 7. Where patients get stuck

Preliminary, unpublished study reported by MOT/Yle (2025): average days from first symptoms to first treatment.

| Stage | Finland |
|---|---|
| Patient delay | 56 |
| Primary care | 41 |
| Specialist care | **58** |
| **Total** | **155** (Sweden 107, Denmark 67) |

**1. Patient delay (56 days)**
- Early symptoms such as a worsening cough are easy to dismiss.

**2. Primary care (41 days)**
- A normal X-ray with continuing symptoms means nobody follows up unless the patient comes back. Of the regions we reviewed, only KYS gives primary care an instruction for this situation.
- Referral quality varies (mentor: Kaisa). A weak referral gets a lower urgency rating in specialist care.

**3. Specialist care (58 days, the longest stage)**
- Triage is based only on the referral text.
- A returned referral, or a request for more information, restarts the loop. The patient may not even know this has happened.

**4. Parallel routes**
- Occupational health gets patients to specialist care faster (mentor: Hanna).
- Private-sector referrals are not always accepted by public hospitals.
- When occupational health ends, the patient has to make sure follow-up continues.

**5. Nobody sees the whole picture**
- No cancer quality registry.
- Timestamps for referral, first visit and diagnosis exist but are not reported or monitored.

## 8. What this means for Lingonberry

Our alert rules should target the handovers. Thresholds are drafts to confirm with Kaisa.

| Handover | Draft alert rule |
|---|---|
| Referral returned or more information requested | No new referral within 7 days → alert |
| Referral accepted | No first visit within 14 days → alert (Keski-Suomi's own target is 2–3 weeks) |
| Symptoms recorded, normal X-ray | No referral and no follow-up visit within X days → alert |

**Core message:** The timestamps exist but nobody monitors them, and the law sets no deadline for a cancer suspicion. Until national target times are in place, an alert makes the delay visible and points training at referral quality.
